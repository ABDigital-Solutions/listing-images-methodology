# Compliance

Run after the seven craft gates. A prompt that passes every craft gate can still be illegal.

**This is not legal advice.** It is a checklist for catching the obvious problems before they reach
someone qualified. Anything comparative, or any health-adjacent claim, should be reviewed by a lawyer
before it goes live.

---

## Marketplace — slot 1

- Pure white background · product 85–90 % of frame
- No props, no text overlays, no badges or graphics outside the packaging
- No borders, watermarks, or logos not on the pack
- Real product; nothing that misrepresents what arrives
- ≥1600 px, 2000 px+ to enable zoom

Gallery slots relax all of this. Slot 1 does not.

## Marketplace — all slots

- No disparaging content about competitors
- No claims the listing text cannot support
- No badges implying certification you do not hold
- No before/after that implies an outcome you cannot evidence

---

## Comparative advertising

Any image contrasting your product with "the others" enters regulated territory.

**In the EU / Germany** — comparative advertising is lawful under **§ 6 UWG** and Directive
2006/114/EC, but it must be objective, verifiable and relevant, and it must not **denigrate**
(*Herabsetzung*). An image implying competitors ship contaminated goods can be read as denigrating
even without naming anyone.

**The reframe that sidesteps it entirely:** compare against **your own process**, not against a
competitor.

| Risky | Safe |
|---|---|
| our seed vs. their seed | before cleaning vs. after cleaning — *our* material |
| us vs. the market | against a published standard, or against the legal limit |
| generic "inferior" competitor product | the fraction *we* sort out |

The reframe is usually the *better* image too: it shows work rather than throwing an insult, and the
claim is one you can evidence from your own process records.

Also check magnitude here, not only in Gate 2. A comparison that is directionally true but
exaggerated in the image is still misleading.

## Health and food claims

Strictest category. In the EU only claims on the **authorised list** may be used, in the authorised
wording.

Never depict:

- Medical symbols, pills, capsules, syringes, lab coats implying pharmaceutical authority
- Weight-loss implication — before/after body shots, measuring tapes, scales
- Disease prevention or treatment of any kind
- A health outcome as a visual metaphor (a heart, a "boost", an energy burst)

**Nutrition claims** ("high in fibre", "source of omega-3") have legal thresholds. Verify against the
actual nutrition panel before any of it appears in an image, including in overlay text.

---

## Numbers in images

Every figure that appears in an image is a claim, and it is harder to correct than body copy once
the listing is live.

- Net weight, portion counts, ratios, percentages, durations
- Trace each to the fact base
- **An unverified number must not ship.** Mark it and block the image rather than "verify later" —
  later does not happen

Common offenders: *"reaches for X portions"*, *"stays fresh for X days"*, *"X % pure"*,
*"swells to X times"*.

---

## Identity and provenance

- Origin claims (country, region) must match the actual supply chain, including when it changes
- Certification marks — Bio, EU organic, fair trade — only if currently held, in correct artwork
- Never generate a certification seal. Composite the real artwork file
- Awards and test results need a date and a source

**Never let a model draw a certification mark.** It produces a plausible-looking fake, which is a
serious problem and an easy one to miss at a glance.
